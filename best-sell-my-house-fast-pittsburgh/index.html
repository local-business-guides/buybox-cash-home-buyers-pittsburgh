A short checklist before you call

You don’t need a polished listing or a folder of paperwork to start a conversation. It can help to have a few basics in mind:

  • The property address and the best way to reach you.
  • Your preferred timeframe and any date you’re working around.
  • What you know about the home’s condition, including repairs you’ve put off.
  • Whether belongings or unwanted items will remain in the home.
  • Any questions about how the sale would work or what happens next.

If you don’t know an answer, say so. A straightforward conversation can help clarify what details matter without obligating you to move forward.

Mistakes that can make a sale harder

Don’t assume you have to fix every visible problem before speaking with a buyer. Repairs and preparation may be unnecessary for a cash sale, so ask before spending time or money. Also avoid agreeing to a timeline until you understand each step and feel ready to proceed. If you’re comparing options, write down what each buyer says about timing, property preparation, and the next decision you’ll need to make.

Another common mistake is leaving practical concerns until the last minute. If you’re sorting through a home after a major change, or aren’t sure what can stay behind, bring that up early. A buyer can explain how those details fit into the conversation.
